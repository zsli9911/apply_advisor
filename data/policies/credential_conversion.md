# Credential & Grade Conversion for European Applications (学历与成绩换算)

> Illustrative guide. Conversion practices differ by institution — always confirm with the
> admissions office or the official credential evaluator (e.g. ENIC-NARIC).

## Converting Chinese percentage grades to a 4.0 GPA

Most French and European admissions teams accept a standard conversion from the Chinese
100-point scale: 90–100 ≈ 4.0, 85–89 ≈ 3.7, 80–84 ≈ 3.3, 75–79 ≈ 3.0, 70–74 ≈ 2.7,
60–69 ≈ 2.0. Some universities instead ask for the official average ("均分") plus a
ranking certificate. If your transcript uses a 5.0 scale, a linear conversion
(score / 5 × 4) is the common baseline. Always send the ORIGINAL scale transcript plus
the conversion — do not convert silently.

## Recognition of 3-year bachelor degrees (三年制本科)

Many European master's programs formally require a 4-year bachelor (240 ECTS
equivalent). Practice varies:

- France: most universities evaluate case by case via Campus France; a strong 3-year
  degree (e.g. UK/India/three-year college) is often accepted for master's admission,
  sometimes with a required "mise à niveau" (bridging) semester.
- Netherlands & Germany: research universities frequently require 240 ECTS / 4 years;
  universities of applied sciences are more flexible.
- A recognised credential evaluation (ENIC-NARIC statement of comparability) strengthens
  a 3-year-degree application significantly.

If a program page says "4-year bachelor required" and yours is 3-year, contact
admissions BEFORE applying and attach the equivalence statement.

## Degree equivalence paperwork

Prepare: final transcript (all years), graduation certificate, degree certificate, and
certified translations into English or French. Chinese applicants usually also provide
CDGDC/CHESICC verification reports; many schools now request electronic verification
directly.

## Ranking and school-tier context (院校背景)

Selective programs read your GPA in the context of your institution (985/211/double
non-key in China, or international equivalents). A lower GPA from a top-tier school with
strong research output can outweigh a higher GPA elsewhere — mention national/major
ranking certificates if favourable.
