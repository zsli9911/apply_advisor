# Health Insurance, Housing and Living Costs

> Illustrative reference document for the demo knowledge base.

## Student health insurance in France

Most international students must register with the French national student health insurance
(via the ameli.fr student portal) shortly after arriving. Registration is free for students
enrolled in higher education. A top-up private insurance ("mutuelle") is optional and covers
the part not reimbursed by the public system.

## Housing options

- CROUS university residences: the most affordable, but limited and competitive.
- Private student residences: more availability, higher rent.
- Shared flats ("colocation") and studios on the private market.

## CAF housing aid

Students in France, including many international students, can apply for housing assistance
("APL") from the CAF (Caisse d'Allocations Familiales) once they have a lease and a French
bank account. The aid reduces monthly rent and is means-tested.

## Budgeting

Living costs vary strongly by city. Paris and Swiss cities are the most expensive; regional
French cities such as Grenoble, Lyon, or Strasbourg are notably cheaper. A common planning
figure for France outside Paris is roughly 800–1,000 EUR per month including rent, food,
transport, and insurance; Paris and Switzerland run considerably higher.

## Bank account and phone

Open a French bank account early — it is needed for CAF aid, rent, and many subscriptions.
A "RIB" (bank details document) is frequently requested by administrations.
